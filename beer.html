    Our Brewers

    Science and heart, in equal measure.

    Jeff brings the Monterey Bay. Gino brings the still. Together they science and heart the hell out of every batch.

    Jeff has spent fifteen years between barrels and vines, with a palate shaped by Carmel Valley fruit and coastal fog. Gino came up the honest way: a homebrew kit, a production brewhouse, and now the patience of the still. One reads a beer like a winemaker. The other reads it like a distiller.

    In our cellar they measure everything. Gravity, temperature, the minute the hops go in, the day the yeast is done talking. Then they taste, argue, laugh and adjust, because numbers can tell you a beer is correct, but only people can tell you it's good.

    Science gets every batch to the line. Heart carries it over. That's the whole recipe, and it's the same one we use for everything else in this building.

    13,000 Years of Beer

    Brewing is older than writing, and it has always been about gathering. We're just the latest stop on a very long timeline.

    ~11,000 BCE13,000 years ago

    A brew in the cave

    Residue in stone mortars at Raqefet Cave near Mount Carmel suggests the Natufian people were brewing a grain based, beer-like drink, likely for feasts honoring the dead. It's the oldest known evidence of brewing.

    ~3500 BCEGodin Tepe, Iran

    Beerstone in the jars

    Chemical traces of barley beer found in clay jars in the Zagros Mountains are some of the earliest proof of beer as an everyday drink.

    ~2500 BCEGiza, Egypt

    Paid in pints

    The crews who built the pyramids received daily beer rations, often cited at around four to five liters a day. Thick, nourishing and safer than the water.

    ~1800 BCESumer

    A hymn that's a recipe

    The Hymn to Ninkasi, goddess of beer, praises her while walking through the steps of brewing: the bappir bread, the soaking malt, the fermenting vat.

    ~1750 BCEBabylon

    Tavern law

    The Code of Hammurabi set rules for beer sellers. A tavern keeper caught short pouring could face drowning. We'll stick to honest pints.

    ~900 CEAthlone, Ireland

    The oldest pub

    Sean's Bar traces its roots to around 900 CE and is widely called Ireland's oldest pub. Same idea as ours: a warm room, a drink and the whole town.

    1040Bavaria

    Monks keep the lights on

    Weihenstephan Abbey is considered the oldest continuously operating brewery in the world. Monasteries brewed for pilgrims and travelers, the original brewpubs.

    ~1150Rhineland

    Hops arrive

    Abbess Hildegard of Bingen wrote that hops' bitterness keeps a drink from spoiling. Before hops, brewers flavored beer with gruit, a blend of herbs.

    1516Bavaria

    The purity law

    The Reinheitsgebot limited beer to water, barley and hops. Yeast wasn't on the list because no one knew it existed yet.

    1982West Coast

    Brewpubs come back

    After Prohibition, brewing and selling beer under one roof was largely illegal. Yakima, Washington got the first modern American brewpub in 1982, and Mendocino Brewing opened California's first in Hopland in 1983.
